> [!definition]
> **Cardinal utility theory** gives meaning to differences between utility levels, beyond the ranking of consumption bundles. On a chosen utility scale, $u(y)-u(x)$ describes the utility gain from bundle $x$ to bundle $y$.

## Interpretation

Utility numbers depend on the chosen origin and unit. A difference between two utility levels can be compared with another difference; a ratio of the *levels* does not have the same meaning. Thus, $u(y)=20$ and $u(x)=10$ do not by themselves mean that $y$ provides “twice as much satisfaction” as $x$.

## Derivation

Changing the origin and unit of a cardinal scale gives a positive affine transformation:

$$
v(x)=a u(x)+b,\qquad a>0.
$$

The transformation multiplies every utility difference by the same positive number:

$$
v(y)-v(x)=a\bigl[u(y)-u(x)\bigr].
$$

Consequently, comparisons between utility gains are preserved, even though the reported utility levels change.

## Connections

- A preference relation records which bundle is at least as good as another. That ranking alone does not determine the size of a utility gain.
- [[Ordinal Utility Theory]] uses utility numbers only to represent that ranking. It allows any strictly increasing transformation, including ones that change comparisons between utility differences.
