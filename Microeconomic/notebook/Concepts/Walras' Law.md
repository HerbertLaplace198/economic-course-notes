In consumer theory, **Walras' Law** says that a consumer who maximizes a locally non-satiated preference relation spends the entire budget. For prices $p$, income $m$, and an optimal bundle $x(p,m)$,

$$
p\cdot x(p,m)=m.
$$

The budget set initially requires only $p\cdot x\leq m$. If an optimum had $p\cdot x<m$, there would be room to choose a nearby, affordable bundle. Local non-satiation says that a better bundle exists arbitrarily nearby, contradicting optimality.

In a **pure exchange economy**, consumer $h$ owns endowment $\omega^h$ and has income $p\cdot\omega^h$. Summing consumers' budget equalities gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
p\cdot z(p)
&=p\cdot\sum_h\bigl[x^h(p,p\cdot\omega^h)-\omega^h\bigr]\\[0.8em]
&=\sum_h\bigl[p\cdot x^h(p,p\cdot\omega^h)-p\cdot\omega^h\bigr]
=0,
\end{aligned}
$$

where $z(p)$ is **aggregate excess demand**. Thus, the *value* of aggregate excess demand is zero; individual markets can still have nonzero excess demand. If all but one market clear and the remaining good has a positive price, that final market must clear too.
